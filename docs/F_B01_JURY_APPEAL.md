# Replay-origin jury accounting (F-B01)

A leader replay after an Undetermined decision can end with a ValidatorsTimeout
decision. The replay's completed leader work has the `LEADER_TIMEOUT_50_PERCENT`
fee color, but the next admitted appeal is a fresh validator jury. The fee
color does not change that admission into a leader-timeout appeal.

The focused forty-validator, leader-unit 100, validator-unit 200 fixture now
quotes a 2,600 jury bond. If that thirteen-seat jury confirms the timeout
decision, seven aligned jurors receive 742 each from the 5,200 pool of jury
fees and forfeited bond, with 6 remaining from integer division. If it
overturns the decision, the appellant receives 6,500 (2,600 principal plus
3,900 profit), seven jurors receive 200 each, and two prior voters receive
200 vindication credits. The overturned replay is skipped; a terminal normal
remedy uses normal fees, while a true leader timeout remedy keeps its actual
LT50 payout. An appeal against an actual timed-out leader still follows the
leader-timeout family and retains its LT150 control.

`consensus_vectors/f_b01_replay_origin_jury.json` is an additive parity
sidecar generated by `python3 scripts/07_generate_consensus_vectors.py
--f-b01-only --output-dir consensus_vectors`. Its generated cases include
the source path, immutable case ID, admitted route and bond, round labels,
every round's final rotation as ordered raw address/ballot seats, its leader
address and action, majority, graph source classification and admission route,
bookkeeping-only flag, seat-indexed jury credits, vindication credits,
distinct funders, appellant principal and profit, division dust, and remedy
label. The raw ballots preserve the input for independent Consensus
recorder/processor replay; the source classification records the intended
semantic transition even for a leader appeal whose ballots are `NA`.

Each appeal row also includes the bond, payer, and payout observed in the
simulator's appeal FeeEvents. The first leader replay costs 2,300 in the mixed
path (100 leader plus eleven 200 validator units) and pays its appellant 5,750;
the later validator jury costs 2,600 and pays its appellant 6,500 on success.
The actual leader-timeout control costs 1,100 and pays its appellant 2,750.
The manual all-idle jury's 2,600 `appealPayout` is its explicit principal
refund expectation, not an output of the generic idleness prepass. The
`remedyAwards` record lists
the final round's leader credit, every validator seat's credit including
zeroes, and any round-local sender credit from payout events. The normal
remedy pays its leader 100 and twelve validator seats 200 each; the actual
timeout remedy pays its leader 50 and no validator credits. The true
leader-timeout control pays its leader 150, three validator seats 200 each,
and the sender 50 in that round. All monetary fields are decimal strings.
The simulator's combined leader ballot produces both a leader and a
validator FeeEvent for seat zero. `roundInputs` retains that raw combined
ballot, and parity checks must compare both role credits for that address.

The all-idle no-reveal case is explicitly marked as a manual admission/refund
fixture. Its 2,600 bond quote comes from the generated admitted jury case,
its first three rounds' inputs are copied from that generated case, and its
thirteen jury ballots are manually set to `IDLE`. The expected principal
refund represents the existing E-B03 rule. The
generic simulator idleness prepass removes all idle votes before labeling and
cannot independently settle that path. This sidecar does not claim that it
does, and it makes no early-depletion policy decision.
